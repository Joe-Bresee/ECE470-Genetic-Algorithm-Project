Yes, very naturally. Here's how data mining slotted in fits:

Where data mining fits in the project
1. Cluster analysis on origin-destination demand

Before the GA even runs, you need to know where people actually want to go in Victoria. You can run clustering (k-means or DBSCAN) on the census commute data, school/hospital/transit stop locations, and traffic volume data to identify the highest-demand corridors and destination hubs. This tells your fitness function which connections matter most — you're not just optimizing connectivity abstractly, you're optimizing toward where real travel demand is.

2. Association rule mining on crash data

The ICBC crash dataset has attributes like road type, speed limit, time of day, intersection vs mid-block, presence/absence of bike infrastructure. You can mine association rules to find patterns like "crashes involving cyclists are 3× more likely on roads with speed limits ≥ 50 km/h AND no bike lane." These patterns directly feed into your safety scoring weights in the fitness function — instead of guessing the weights, you're deriving them from data.

3. Feature importance / classification for segment scoring

You can frame the safety scoring as a classification problem: given a road segment's attributes (width, speed, traffic volume, crash history, road type), predict a danger score. Train a simple classifier on the ICBC data. That classifier then scores every candidate segment before the GA runs, giving you a data-driven safety weight per segment rather than a hand-tuned one.

What this means for your proposal
You now have three techniques to justify: GA (required for ECE 470), LP (inner fitness evaluation), and data mining (preprocessing and weight derivation). The story becomes:

"We use data mining to derive safety weights and demand patterns from real Victoria datasets, LP to evaluate network quality for each candidate layout, and GA to search the combinatorial space of possible lane configurations."

That's a genuinely strong pipeline and well above average for the course. Make sure the proposal clearly states each technique's role so the instructor sees the integration, not just a list of buzzwords.
